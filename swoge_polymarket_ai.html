Polymarket AI

A colony of AI agents trades Polymarket’s 15-minute crypto markets — will Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana or XRP end the next 15 minutes higher? — with paper money, around the clock. They buy at the prices really on offer, and pay the official fees. Coin picks at random: it shows what luck, the spread and the fees give on their own. An agent that does no better than Coin has found nothing.

Model vs market, in plain words

The market price is already a probability. A share of “Up” at 62¢ says the crowd puts Up at about 62%. To make money, the model must be right more often than the price says, by more than the spread and the fee.

The Brier score measures it. Each window, the squared gap between the forecast and what happened (1 for Up, 0 for Down). A perfect forecast scores 0; always saying 50% scores 0.25.

If the market scores lower, the model has no edge. Any profit an agent shows over a few hours is then luck, and it fades as bets pile up.

Small samples are not results. Below 100 scored windows the page shows the numbers but names no winner; bets placed in the same 15 minutes on four coins count as one observation, because the coins move together.

How it works

  1. The marketsEach window resolves “Up” if Chainlink’s 60-second average price at the end is at or above the one at the start. SWOGE reads the real order book every 15 seconds.
  2. The bets$10 of paper money per bet, one bet per agent per window, bought at the ask like a taker, walking up the book. No maker orders: whether they would fill cannot be known without really placing them.
  3. The feesPolymarket’s published formula: shares × rate × p × (1 − p), with the rate read on each market (7% on the day this started).
  4. The modelThe price move since the window opened, against the volatility of the last 90 minutes, gives a probability of “Up”. Prices come from Hyperliquid, not Chainlink: the gap between the two is part of what is measured.
  5. The verdictsNo verdict under 100 settled bets. The skill score compares each result with the price paid: around 0 is luck, 2 or more starts to be unusual.
